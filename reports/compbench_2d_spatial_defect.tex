\section*{Summary}

The released T2I-CompBench 2D-spatial evaluator resolves a prompt's two object nouns to detector
outputs by \emph{exact string equality} against a fixed 722-entry label vocabulary. Many of the
vocabulary's entries are composite strings inherited from the unified UniDet label space
(\code{'cattle, cow'}, \code{'mouse2, mouse'}, \code{'table, desk'}), and some ordinary nouns have no
entry at all. When either noun fails to match, the score is forced to zero before the image is
consulted.

\textbf{318 of the 1{,}000 prompts in the official \code{spatial.txt} cannot receive a nonzero score
for any image, from any model.} Nine nouns account for all of them. On a labelled pool of 298
prompts $\times$ 8 candidates the condition separates perfectly: all 98 affected prompts score zero
on all 8 candidates, with no exceptions.

The sibling \code{3D\_spatial\_eval.py}, in the same directory, resolves nouns by \emph{substring}
containment instead. That single difference is why the 3D category does not show the defect --- and it
introduces the opposite one, since \code{cat} is a substring of \code{'cattle, cow'}.

We rebuild the evaluator with an ontology-aware matcher, verify that our re-implementation reproduces
the released scores exactly on 8{,}642 images, and then ask what changes. Model rankings do
\textbf{not} change in any statistically defensible way. What does change is everything to do with
using the metric as a \emph{target}: repairing the matcher raises the measured best-of-$N$ gain by
29\% (from $+0.2143$ to $+0.2760$), 50 prompts per 298 become selectable that were not before, and
selecting against the released metric forfeits 25\% of the gain that is actually available.

\section{The mechanism}

\subsection{Parsing}
The evaluator takes the prompt from the image filename and extracts two nouns with spaCy. The only
lexical normalisation anywhere in the file is a four-word person list.

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={\code{UniDet\_eval/2D\_spatial\_eval.py}, simple-structure branch.}]
nlp = spacy.load("en_core_web_sm")
doc = nlp(prompt)
obj1 = [token.text for token in doc if token.pos_=='NOUN'][0]
obj2 = [token.text for token in doc if token.pos_=='NOUN'][-1]

person = ['girl','boy','man','woman']
if obj1 in person: obj1 = "person"
if obj2 in person: obj2 = "person"
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{The vocabulary}
Detected class indices are mapped to strings through a fixed list loaded at import:

\begin{lstlisting}
obj_label_map = torch.load('dataset/detection_features.pt')['labels']   # 722 entries
\end{lstlisting}

Of those 722 entries, 64 are comma-joined composites. The atomic names they contain are not
separately addressable, because matching is done against the full string.

\subsection{The gate}
\begin{lstlisting}[caption={The defect. \code{obj} is a \emph{list} of label strings, so \code{in} is
list membership, i.e.\ exact string equality --- not substring containment.}]
obj = []
for i in range(len(obj_bounding_box)):
    obj_name = obj_label_map[obj_labels_dict[i]]
    obj.append(obj_name)
...
if obj1 in obj and obj2 in obj:
    obj1_pos = obj.index(obj1)
    obj2_pos = obj.index(obj2)
    score  = 0.25*instance_score[obj1_pos].item() + 0.25*instance_score[obj2_pos].item()
    score += determine_position(locality, box1, box2) / 2
elif obj1 in obj: score = 0.25*instance_score[obj1_pos].item()
elif obj2 in obj: score = 0.25*instance_score[obj2_pos].item()
else:             score = 0
if (score < 0.5): score = 0
\end{lstlisting}

Two consequences follow directly from this block. First, a failed match on \emph{either} noun caps
the score at $0.25$, which the final threshold then sets to zero: finding one object is worth exactly
as much as finding nothing. Second, the score is a joint gate over detection and relation,
\[
s_{\text{raw}}=0.25\,(c_A+c_B)+0.5\,r(A,B),\qquad
s=\begin{cases} s_{\text{raw}} & s_{\text{raw}}\ge 0.5\\ 0 & \text{otherwise,}\end{cases}
\]
so with typical confidences $c\!\approx\!0.9$ a correct pair of detections and a wrong relation gives
$0.45<0.5$ and is also zeroed. Detection failure, relation failure and \emph{matching} failure are
all reported as the same number.

\subsection{Repairability}
Not every affected prompt can be recovered. Splitting composite labels on commas recovers 115;
a five-entry rename map (\code{sofa}$\to$\code{couch}, \code{bag}$\to$\code{handbag},
\code{phone}$\to$\code{cell phone}, \code{computer}$\to$\code{laptop},
\code{painting}$\to$\code{picture}) recovers a further 147. The remaining 56 all involve
\code{wallet}, which has no class in the vocabulary under any name; those prompts are not
repairable and should be reported as \emph{unsupported} rather than scored zero.

\subsection{A second layer: the metric is near-binary even where it works}
\begin{figure}[htbp]\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.62\linewidth]{figs_compbench/score_distribution.pdf}
\end{figure}
\vspace{-0.3cm}
68.2\% of the 2{,}384 candidate images score exactly zero, and 136 of 298 prompts (45.6\%) are
eight-way ties carrying no candidate-level information at all. Decomposing within-prompt variance
into the zero/nonzero split versus graded variation among nonzero scores gives
$\eta^2=0.9704$ --- and the figure is \emph{identical} when restricted to the 200 matchable prompts,
because the unscoreable prompts contribute no variance either way. So repairing the matcher does not
address this second layer: even where the evaluator works, essentially all of its within-prompt
signal is whether the joint gate was crossed, not how well the relation was realised.

\section{A repaired evaluator, and what changes}

\subsection{Design and validation}
The released script discards the detector's output and keeps one scalar per image, which is why the
defect cannot be diagnosed from cached artifacts. We re-ran UniDet over every evaluation for which
images survive, kept the raw detections, and scored them offline under three matching rules holding
everything else --- weighting, \code{determine\_position}, threshold, and the first-match
\code{.index()} semantics --- identical.

\begin{quote}\small
\textbf{Validation.} Our \code{official} column reproduces each directory's own cached
\code{vqa\_result.json} on \textbf{29/29 directories, 298/298 images each, 8{,}642 images, zero
mismatches.} Any difference in the other columns is therefore attributable to the matching rule alone.
\end{quote}

\subsection{The strongest consequence: the metric mis-optimises}

Selecting the candidate that maximises the \emph{released} metric, then measuring that candidate
under the repaired one, yields $+0.2061$ against the $+0.2760$ actually available:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lr}
\toprule
selection policy & realised gain, measured under the repaired rule \\
\midrule
argmax of the released metric & $+0.2061$ \\
argmax of the repaired metric & $+0.2760$ \\
\midrule
\textbf{forfeited} & \textbf{25.3\%} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Any procedure that uses this metric as a signal --- best-of-$N$ selection, rejection sampling,
GORS-style fine-tuning on high-scoring generations, or a reward model fit to it --- had exactly zero
gradient on a third of its prompts, and we can now put a number on what that costs.

\subsection{What is unaffected}
Prompt-blind selectors built from DINOv2 features do not benefit. The best of them,
\code{quadrant\_pc\_std}, realises $+0.0262$ $[-0.0185,+0.0695]$ under the official rule and
$+0.0380$ $[-0.0126,+0.0844]$ under the repaired one: not significant under either. The repair
neither rescues nor refutes them.

\section{Limitations}

\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
\item \textbf{Rankings survive.} We cannot claim published model comparisons were wrong. The claim is
about absolute scores, effect sizes, and the metric's use as a target.
\item \textbf{One generator.} The labelled pool is SD3.5-Medium. The 318/1{,}000 figure is a property
of the prompts and the evaluator and holds regardless, but the pool statistics are single-model.
\item \textbf{The rename map is a judgement.} Five entries, each a rename rather than a claim that two
categories are close enough. Results under the comma-split-only rule are also reported.
\item \textbf{3D exposure is not an error rate.} Converting 87.3\% exposure into a measured
false-positive rate requires re-running detection on the 3D pool, which we have not done.
\item \textbf{No human validation.} We show the released metric disagrees with the image; we have not
run a human study establishing what the corrected metric should say.
\end{enumerate}
