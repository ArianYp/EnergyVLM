The answer first — four claims, four evidence levels

Sampling compression works

Our \(M1\) control reduces the \(4\)-step fidelity gap dramatically relative to unmodified SD\(3.5\)-M.

Boundary: it is a custom CM/LCM-inspired baseline, not a canonical implementation of recent \(s\mathrm{CM}\) or \(r\mathrm{CM}\).

REPA on \(M1\) promising

At \(4\) steps, \(\operatorname{CMMD}\) moves from \(69.7\) to \(67.4\) while VQAScore holds.

Boundary: one seed and no confidence interval on the CMMD difference.

Pairwise student training works

Correct teacher preferences beat randomized labels on independent CompBench and GenEval2.

Effect: \(+0.04587\) and \(+0.04958\), with both paired \(95\%\) intervals above zero.

MMDiT ranker text read null

It selects better images than random, but correct versus counterfactual text is null on official CompBench.

Boundary: useful image-quality signal, not yet prompt-specific compatibility.

Most promising path: direct pairwise preference training is now the generator path. Keep REPA as the fidelity branch, and use the matched correct-versus-counterfactual label intervention to test whether the gain truly comes from prompt composition.

The ranking objective — every symbol and its origin

labels
\(r_j=\operatorname{VQAScore}(x_j,c)\)
\(\longrightarrow\)
soft target
\(q_j=\operatorname{softmax}_j((r_j-\bar r)/\tau_r)\)
\(\parallel\)
model scores
\(s_j=s_\psi(x_j,c)\)
\(\longrightarrow\)
predicted choice
\(p_j=\operatorname{softmax}_j(s_j)\)
\[ \mathcal L_{\mathrm{rank}} =\underbrace{-\sum_{j=1}^{J}q_j\log p_j}_{\text{ListNet-style soft listwise cross-entropy}} +\lambda_{\mathrm{pair}} \underbrace{\log\!\left(1+e^{-(s_{j^+}-s_{j^-})}\right)}_{\text{RankNet / Bradley--Terry-style best--worst logistic loss}}, \] \[ j^+=\arg\max_j r_j,\qquad j^-=\arg\min_j r_j,\qquad \tau_r=0.05,\qquad \lambda_{\mathrm{pair}}=0.25. \]

The old slide’s \(\operatorname{CE}\) arguments were ambiguous. The code computes \(H(q,p)=-\sum_jq_j\log p_j\). Also, \(r_j-\bar r\) is mathematically redundant because softmax is invariant to a common shift.

Closest work on fast generation and representation fidelity

Consistency family

CM, ICML \(2023\) introduces trajectory consistency. \(s\mathrm{CM}\), ICLR \(2025\) stabilises continuous-time training at scale. \(r\mathrm{CM}\), ICLR \(2026\) adds score regularisation for large T2I and video models.

Representation alignment

REPA, ICLR \(2025\) aligns DiT hidden states to frozen vision features. U-REPA, NeurIPS \(2025\) extends the idea to U-Nets. iREPA, ICLR \(2026\) isolates the role of spatial structure.

Our Phase R

An audited one-variable test of whether clean-image REPA transfers to an already pretrained few-step SD\(3.5\)-M student. The positive \(4\)-step signal is useful, but not yet a mechanism or SOTA claim.

Closest work says the MMDiT projector path is still valid

Diffusion-native reward models

LRM, NeurIPS \(2025\) repurposes diffusion features with Bradley–Terry preference training. SLRM, ICLR \(2026\) uses the complete MMDiT as a noisy-latent discriminator.

DiNa-LRM, ICML \(2026\) is the closest accepted architecture: SD\(3.5\)-M features, timestep conditioning, richer token fusion, and noise-calibrated Thurstone likelihood.

Reward enters generator training

GRAFT / P-GRAFT, ICLR \(2026\) uses top-\(K\)-of-\(M\) reward selection and intermediate distribution shaping. DMSampler, ICML \(2026\) couples a few-step sampler to an RL–redistillation loop.

RTDMD, arXiv \(2026\) combines distribution matching and reward-guided optimisation.

Conclusion from recent literature: our negative result rejects the small CompBench bank, weak pooled readout, and within-prompt-only loss—not the projector idea. The next scorer should use multi-layer tokens, timestep conditioning, pairwise probabilistic training, and explicit wrong-prompt negatives.

Data decision — neither “just COCO” nor “oracle equals label”

Do not

Use COCO alone as the compositional ranking dataset. Captions provide pairs, not within-prompt preferences or counterfactual correctness.

Do not

Treat a stronger “oracle” generator as its own judge. It supplies diverse candidates; a separate evaluator must label prompt satisfaction.

Keep untouched

GenEval, GenEval2, and final T2I-CompBench variants. Hold out concept combinations and generator families, not merely exact prompt strings.

Supervisor takeaway

What we know, what we do not, and the decision now

\(M1\)

A useful internal compression control built from recognised consistency ingredients, but not yet the reviewer-facing SOTA baseline.

REPA

Promising fidelity protection and a positive CompBench increment when combined with preference training; still needs replicated, correctly matched fidelity evaluation.

Direct preference

The strongest completed result: independent CompBench and GenEval2 both improve over a randomized-label placebo.

Standalone ranker

Better than random reranking, but its correct-versus-counterfactual prompt effect is null under official CompBench.

Decision now: the publication-scale preference \(+\) REPA campaign is launched, but expensive training remains causally gated by the matched label-source evaluation. This gives us the upside of scaling immediately without silently promoting a generic quality prior. Retain a canonical \(s\mathrm{CM}\) or \(r\mathrm{CM}\) reviewer-facing baseline alongside it.
